# RFC: name the relation under `Related items`

## Summary

The plan template's `Items` section becomes `Related items`, and each line
under it says how the item relates to this plan rather than giving a slug
alone. A plan that touches no other item loses the section.

What changes is the shape of a line, not only the heading: a few names in a
row do not say which item has to come first, which one waits, or which run
side by side, and a reader cannot tell from the list what the list is for.

## Motivation

The section holds one line per item today, and what a writer puts on the
line is the slug. Written that way, a section like this one says nothing a
reader can act on:

```
rfc-cache-the-parse
plan-split-the-lexer
bug-lexer-drops-eof
```

Three items are named and no relation is stated. Does the plan wait for the
RFC, or carry it out? Is the bug a reason to do either? The comment under the
section explains what belongs there, but the comment is read after the
heading, and the heading is what a reader uses to decide whether to read the
section at all.

The heading is the smaller half of the problem. `Items` is a word for the
things a backlog holds, so inside a plan it names the wrong level: the
section does not hold items, it holds pointers to other items and the reason
for each pointer. `Related items` says the section is about the relation,
which is what the lines have to carry.

## Design

The section is `## Related items`. Its lines say the item and the relation,
with no fixed vocabulary:

```markdown
- carries out `20261114-rfc-how-items-refer-to-each-other.md`
- waits on that RFC: the file name shape has to be settled first
- runs in parallel; no ordering between them
```

The relation goes on the line in whatever words fit. A plan that relates to
no other item deletes the section, as it does today.

The comment under the section says what the lines are for, and gives the
three cases above as examples rather than as values to choose from. The
`Sections:` line in `explain kinds` follows the new name.

## Alternatives

**Rename the heading and leave the lines alone.** This is the smallest
change and it fixes half the problem. It does not fix the half that a reader
of the section actually trips over: `Related items` followed by three bare
names still leaves the relation to be guessed.

**Fix the vocabulary.** Write down the relations a plan may have -- `carries
out`, `waits on`, `runs in parallel`, `is replaced by` -- and have writers
pick one. The result reads evenly, and the list can be checked. It also makes
the section a field to fill in: a writer with a relation that is not on the
list either squeezes it into the nearest word or leaves the line bare, and
the relations worth writing are exactly the ones nobody thought to list.

**Drop the section and let the steps carry the relations.** A step can say
what it waits for, and a plan with no section is a smaller template. It
moves the ordering out of one place and into the prose of each step, which is
where a reader looking for the order will not think to look.

## Open questions

Is `Related items` the name worth settling on, given that the section may
hold an item this plan replaces as well as one it carries out? "Related" is
vague, and vague is the point -- but it may be too vague to be a heading.

Should the relation be required on every line, or may a line carry only a
name when the relation is obvious from the plan's Goal? The rule above says
required, so that a bare list never appears; that is a rule about what a
reader can rely on, not about what a writer can write.
